This is a guide, not a rulebook. Every colour in your palette can be worn anywhere: clothing, makeup, nail polish, jewellery, eyewear, ties, hats, scarves and beyond. Use this as a reference to understand what works with your natural colouring. Colour analysis is about empowerment, not restriction.

The Warm Autumn palette contains thousands of shades. The swatches here are carefully chosen examples of what to look for: warm undertone, medium intensity, medium value. Your shades are golden and rich, with a tropical kind of warmth. Use these as a visual compass, not a checklist.

Screen tip: For the most accurate colour reference, turn off your blue light filter and set your screen brightness to around 80 percent or higher. On iPhone look for Night Shift under Settings > Display & Brightness. On Samsung look for Eye Comfort Shield under Settings > Display. Samsung screens also show colour more accurately with Screen mode set to Natural rather than Vivid.

Online shopping tip: When unsure about a colour, try asking an AI assistant: "Is [Brand] [Product] in [Colour Name] cool, warm, or neutral toned? Does it suit Warm Autumn?" Keep in mind AI pulls from across the internet and isn't always accurate, since sources can conflict. Always check samples in store when possible.

Lens tints for sunglasses
Amber, brown, or bronze lenses are your easiest options. Olive-green lenses work well too. Avoid grey, blue, or silver mirrored lenses, which can read harsher than your natural colouring.

Cognac and camel are your most useful bag and shoe colours, with chestnut and dark chestnut for darker pieces. A rust or olive bag is a fun change from brown. Avoid: stark black and cool grey, which read heavier than anything in your natural palette.
Scarves, Hats & Wraps
Near-face accessories matter most
Anything worn close to your face has the same impact as a top or blouse. A terracotta scarf, an olive hat, or a teal wrap near your face will all be flattering. Tip: a patterned scarf mixing two or three of your colours, like rust, mustard, and teal, is an easy way to pull an outfit together.
Patterns & Fabrics
Some ideas to look for
Earthy and natural, with a lighter, livelier touch. Think leaves, botanicals, paisley, plaid, and other shapes from nature. Matte and textured fabrics like suede, corduroy, knits, and tweed look great on you. These are just a few ideas. If a pattern is made up of your shades, it's worth trying.

For the Men
Ties, pocket squares & accessories
The same palette applies. A golden teal or rust tie is a classic, versatile choice. Camel, walnut, and navy suits are strong bases. Hollyhock and Dijon make fun pocket squares if you want a little more personality. Gold or bronze cufflinks over bright silver. An ivory cream shirt over stark white. A peacock or paprika tie over anything icy grey.

Foundation & Tinted Moisturiser
Foundation rule: warm or golden undertone.
What to look for
This applies across every skin tone and depth. Look for shades described as warm, golden, or yellow-based. Avoid anything described as pink or cool. Tip: swatch on your jawline in natural light. A match that blends seamlessly is right; one that pulls noticeably pink or grey is off.
Eyes
Your eyes look great in golden browns, bronzes, and olive greens. A soft eye works beautifully with a stronger lip, and a stronger eye pairs well with a quieter lip.
Eyeshadow: what works
Base eyeshadow: ivory cream, golden beige, light caramel, apricot. Depth: cognac, chestnut, golden olive, plum. Accents: copper, honey gold, turquoise, peacock. Bronze and copper metallics look especially good. Avoid: cool grey, icy silver, and cool lavender.
Eyeliner
Brown, olive, or dark teal are your most versatile liners, each giving a different depth. Tip: a softly smudged brown liner with a terracotta lip is an easy everyday look.
Mascara
Brown or brown-black mascara are your best choices. A very cool-toned black can feel harsh against your golden colouring.
Brows & Beard
Brow & beard guidance
Match your brow product to your natural hair colour or go one shade darker. Look for golden or warm brown, or an auburn tone if your hair has red in it. Avoid anything cool-grey or cool taupe. Tip: ask for "warm brown" or "golden brown" when shopping brow pencils or powders.
Hair Colour
Your most beautiful hair colour is often the one you were born with. Warmth in this placement can show up differently for different people: it might come through more in a beard than the rest of the hair, or read more or less golden depending on the light, so treat this as a general direction rather than a fixed rule.
Your best hair colours
Your naturals: golden blonde, strawberry blonde, auburn, copper, and golden brown through dark brown, kept warm.
What to ask your colourist
Ask for "warm toned," "golden," or "copper," or show them your palette directly. Tip: when in doubt, go warmer rather than cooler.
